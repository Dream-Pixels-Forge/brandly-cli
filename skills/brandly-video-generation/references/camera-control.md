# Camera Control Reference

Complete guide to camera techniques for video generation.

## Shot Types

### Establishing
**Use:** Opening scene, set location
**Description:** Wide shot that establishes the scene
**Syntax:** `[establishing shot]`
**Example:** `[establishing shot] of modern office building at sunrise`

### Medium
**Use:** Standard framing, dialogue
**Description:** Waist-level framing, most common
**Syntax:** `[medium shot]`
**Example:** `[medium shot] of person presenting product`

### Close-Up
**Use:** Emotional moments, detail
**Description:** Face or object filling frame
**Syntax:** `[close-up]` or `[CU]`
**Example:** `[close-up] of hands holding product`

### Extreme Close-Up
**Use:** Macro details, texture
**Description:** Extreme detail, very tight framing
**Syntax:** `[extreme close-up]` or `[ECU]`
**Example:** `[extreme close-up] of product texture`

### Low Angle
**Use:** Heroic, powerful perspective
**Description:** Camera below subject, looking up
**Syntax:** `[low angle]`
**Example:** `[low angle] of sports car`

### High Angle
**Use:** God's eye view, vulnerability
**Description:** Camera above subject, looking down
**Syntax:** `[high angle]`
**Example:** `[high angle] of dining table setup`

### Tracking
**Use:** Follow subject movement
**Description:** Camera follows subject
**Syntax:** `[tracking shot]`
**Example:** `[tracking shot] following person walking`

### Orbital
**Use:** 360 reveal, dynamic
**Description:** Camera orbits around subject
**Syntax:** `[orbital shot]`
**Example:** `[orbital shot] around product`

### Static Product
**Use:** Clean showcase
**Description:** Locked camera, subject moves
**Syntax:** `[static product shot]`
**Example:** `[static product shot] of rotating item`

### POV
**Use:** Immersive experience
**Description:** Point-of-view perspective
**Syntax:** `[POV shot]`
**Example:** `[POV shot] of unboxing product`

## Camera Moves

### Push In
**Use:** Emphasize, focus attention
**Description:** Move toward subject
**Syntax:** `[Push in]`
**Example:** `[Push in] toward product`

### Pull Out
**Use:** Reveal, context
**Description:** Move away from subject
**Syntax:** `[Pull out]`
**Example:** `[Pull out] to reveal full scene`

### Pan
**Use:** Follow horizontal movement
**Description:** Rotate camera horizontally
**Syntax:** `[Pan left]` or `[Pan right]`
**Example:** `[Pan right] following subject`

### Tilt
**Use:** Follow vertical movement
**Description:** Rotate camera vertically
**Syntax:** `[Tilt up]` or `[Tilt down]`
**Example:** `[Tilt up] showing building height`

### Tracking
**Use:** Follow subject movement
**Description:** Camera moves with subject
**Syntax:** `[Tracking shot]`
**Example:** `[Tracking shot] alongside running subject`

### Static
**Use:** Observation, documentary
**Description:** Locked camera, no movement
**Syntax:** `[Static shot]`
**Example:** `[Static shot] of product on display`

### Orbital
**Use:** Dynamic reveal
**Description:** Camera orbits subject
**Syntax:** `[Orbital]`
**Example:** `[Orbital] around hero product`

### Crane
**Use:** Dramatic reveal
**Description:** Vertical rise or lower
**Syntax:** `[Crane up]` or `[Crane down]`
**Example:** `[Crane up] revealing landscape`

### Whip Pan
**Use:** Fast transition
**Description:** Quick horizontal swing
**Syntax:** `[Whip pan]`
**Example:** `[Whip pan] between two subjects`

### Dolly
**Use:** Smooth movement
**Description:** Physical movement closer/farther
**Syntax:** `[Dolly in]` or `[Dolly out]`
**Example:** `[Dolly in] toward subject`

```
[tracking shot] following product, [low angle],

```

## Best Practices

1. **Start wide, go close** — Establish scene, then focus
2. **Match move to mood** — Slow moves for calm, fast for energy
3. **Use orbits for reveals** — 360 degree shows all angles
4. **Track movement** — Follow natural motion
5. **Keep it smooth** — Unless intentional shake for style
