# Scene Construction Reference

## The Scene Equation

Every scene = A character wants something + faces resistance + leaves changed (or changes the situation).

```
SCENE = WANT + RESISTANCE + TURNING POINT
```

If any element is missing, the scene doesn't work.

---

## Scene Anatomy

### 1. Scene Setup (First 10%)

- Who is in the scene?
- What do they want? (specific, observable)
- What's in their way?
- What's the emotional temperature?

### 2. Scene Development (Middle 80%)

- Character pursues want
- Resistance increases
- Stakes escalate
- Something new is revealed

### 3. Scene Turn (Last 10%)

- The situation has changed
- Character has gained or lost something
- The audience knows something they didn't before
- The next scene is now inevitable

---

## The Five Types of Scenes

### 1. Action Scene
- Character pursues external goal
- Physical obstacles
- Clear success/failure
- Example: Naval battle, sniper duel, chase

### 2. Reaction Scene
- Character processes what just happened
- Emotional response
- Decision about what to do next
- Example: Marcus sees the third intelligence, processes the threat

### 3. Revelation Scene
- New information is revealed
- Reframes what the audience thought they knew
- Changes the direction of the story
- Example: "YOU ARE BOTH VERY PREDICTABLE"

### 4. Decision Scene
- Character must choose
- The choice reveals character
- The choice has consequences
- Example: Marcus decides to shoot the node, not Lee

### 5. Transition Scene
- Moves characters from A to B
- Brief, functional
- Can carry mood/subtext
- Example: Character walks from one room to another, processing

## Subtext in Scenes

### What Subtext Is

The gap between what a character says and what they mean. The gap between what they do and why they do it.

### How to Write Subtext

**Level 1: What they SAY**
"I'm fine."

**Level 2: What they MEAN**
"I'm not fine, but I won't show weakness."

**Level 3: What they're REALLY saying**
"I need you to notice I'm not fine, but I need you to not ask."

**Level 4: What they DO instead**
They pour a drink. They check their phone. They stare out the window.

### Subtext Techniques

| Technique | Example | What It Reveals |
|-----------|---------|-----------------|
| Deflection | "How are you?" → "Did you see the game?" | Avoidance, hidden pain |
| Overreaction | Small comment → big reaction | Hidden wound triggered |
| Silence | No response to a question | Refusal, processing, control |
| Physical action | Slamming door, pouring drink | Emotion they won't verbalize |
| Indirect speech | Talking about "someone" who is clearly them | Self-awareness without confession |
| Contradiction | "I don't care" + checking phone | Internal conflict |

## Escalation Patterns

### The Reversal

Every time the protagonist seems to win, something must go wrong.

```
Win → Loss → Win → Loss → Win → Loss → FINAL WIN/LOSS
```

### The Tightening Spiral

Each cycle of the story goes deeper into the same problem, but at higher stakes.

```
Cycle 1: Small conflict, personal stakes
Cycle 2: Bigger conflict, professional stakes
Cycle 3: Largest conflict, life-or-death stakes
```

### The Mirror Scene

A scene that deliberately echoes an earlier scene, but with a crucial difference.

**Example:**
- Scene 1: Marcus types "WELCOME" to Lee. Amber glow. Control.
- Scene 20: Marcus types "ROUND TWO?" to Lee. Amber+Blue glow. Alliance.

Same action. Different meaning. The audience feels the transformation.

## Scene-Level Beat Sheet

For each scene, identify these beats:

1. **Entry state** — How does the scene begin? What's the emotional baseline?
2. **Want** — What does the character want in this specific scene?
3. **Obstacle** — What prevents them from getting it?
4. **Escalation** — How does the obstacle increase?
5. **Turning point** — What changes? What's revealed? What decision is made?
6. **Exit state** — How does the scene end? What's different from the entry state?

## Scene Pacing

### Fast Pacing (Action/Tension)
- Short sentences
- Quick cuts between characters/locations
- Minimal description
- Dialogue is clipped
- Beats are rapid

### Slow Pacing (Emotion/Reflection)
- Long descriptions
- Lingering on details
- Silence between dialogue
- Internal monologue
- Beats are deliberate

### The Pace Map

```
Scene 1: SLOW (establish world)
Scene 2: MEDIUM (introduce character)
Scene 3: FAST (catalyst/disruption)
Scene 4: SLOW (reaction/processing)
Scene 5: FAST (break into two)
Scene 6-12: MEDIUM-FAST (escalation)
Scene 13: SLOW (midpoint reflection)
Scene 14-18: FAST (bad guys close in)
Scene 19: SLOW (all is lost)
Scene 20: SLOW (dark night)
Scene 21: MEDIUM (break into three)
Scene 22-24: FAST (finale)
Scene 25: SLOW (final image)
```

## Scene Checklist

For every scene, verify:

- [ ] A character wants something (specific, observable)
- [ ] There is resistance (obstacle, conflict, opposition)
- [ ] There is escalation (stakes rise, tension increases)
- [ ] There is a turning point (something changes)
- [ ] The scene drives the story forward (can't be removed without consequence)
- [ ] Character is revealed through behavior (not description)
- [ ] Dialogue has subtext (characters don't say what they mean)
- [ ] The pacing matches the emotional function
- [ ] Camera/audio notes are included (for AI filmmaking)
- [ ] The exit state differs from the entry state
