# Beat Sheets and Story Structures Reference

## Save the Cat Beat Sheet (Blake Snyder)

The definitive pacing tool for commercial screenplays. 15 beats with target page numbers for a 110-page script.

### The 15 Beats

| # | Beat | Page | Duration | Purpose |
|---|------|------|----------|---------|
| 1 | Opening Image | 1 | 1-3 pp | Snapshot of protagonist's world BEFORE the story. Visual thesis. |
| 2 | Theme Stated | 5 | 3-6 pp | Someone states the theme, often to the protagonist who doesn't understand it yet. |
| 3 | Setup | 1-10 | 6-12 pp | Establish protagonist's life, relationships, flaws, desires. Show what's missing. |
| 4 | Catalyst | 12 | 1-3 pp | The inciting incident. Something disrupts the status quo irreversibly. |
| 5 | Debate | 12-25 | 10-13 pp | Protagonist hesitates. Fear, doubt, uncertainty. "Should I go?" |
| 6 | Break into Two | 25 | 1-3 pp | Protagonist commits to the journey. Enters the "new world." |
| 7 | B Story | 30 | 2-4 pp | Subplot kicks in — often romantic. Carries the theme. |
| 8 | Fun and Games | 30-55 | 20-25 pp | The "promise of the premise." What the audience came to see. |
| 9 | Midpoint | 55 | 1-3 pp | False victory or false defeat. Stakes are raised. |
| 10 | Bad Guys Close In | 55-75 | 15-20 pp | External and internal pressures mount. Team fractures. |
| 11 | All Is Lost | 75 | 1-3 pp | The worst moment. "Whiff of death." Something must die. |
| 12 | Dark Night of the Soul | 75-85 | 5-10 pp | Protagonist wallows, confronts internal flaw, finds clarity. |
| 13 | Break into Three | 85 | 1-3 pp | New insight. A and B stories merge. The solution emerges. |
| 14 | Finale | 85-110 | 20-25 pp | Protagonist executes the plan, confronts antagonist, transforms. |
| 15 | Final Image | 110 | 1-3 pp | Mirror of Opening Image. Shows how protagonist has changed. |

### Snyder's 10 Story Types

1. **Monster in the House** — Monster + Sin + Confined Space (Alien, The Conjuring)
2. **Golden Fleece** — Road movie, protagonist changes along the journey (Finding Nemo, Speed)
3. **Out of the Bottle** — Wish fulfillment, magic changes life (Big, Liar Liar)
4. **Dude with a Problem** — Ordinary person in extraordinary situation (Die Hard, Titanic)
5. **Rites of Passage** — Life crisis, protagonist must change (Groundhog Day, American Beauty)
6. **Buddy Love** — Two halves become one whole (When Harry Met Sally, Thelma & Louise)
7. **Whydunit** — The investigation, the detective (The Usual Suspects, Chinatown)
8. **The Fool Triumphant** — Underdog vs. the system (Forrest Gump, The Shawshank Redemption)
9. **Institutionalized** — Group vs. individual, ensemble (The Godfather, Office Space)
10. **Superhero** — Ordinary person cursed with power (Spider-Man, Edward Scissorhands)

### The Formula

```
Concept = Concept + Protagonist + Goal + Obstacle + Stakes
Logline = Protagonist + Goal + Obstacle (in one sentence)
```

## Dan Harmon's Story Circle

Simplified Hero's Journey. 8 steps arranged in a circle. Best for TV, character studies, short films.

### The 8 Steps

```
        1. YOU (Comfort Zone)
           |
    8.     |     2. NEED
    CHANGE |     |
           |     3. GO
    7.     |     
    RETURN |     4. SEARCH
           |
        6. TAKE ← 5. FIND
```

**Top half = Known world. Bottom half = Unknown world.**

1. **You** — A character is in a zone of comfort
2. **Need** — But they want something
3. **Go** — They enter an unfamiliar situation
4. **Search** — Adapt to it
5. **Find** — Get what they wanted
6. **Take** — Pay a heavy price for it
7. **Return** — Return to their familiar situation
8. **Change** — Having changed

### Fractal Application

The circle operates at every scale:
- **Entire series** (Breaking Bad: Walter White's transformation)
- **Single season**
- **Single episode** (Rick and Morty applies this per episode)
- **Single scene** (character enters comfortable, wants something, faces resistance, leaves changed)

## The Hero's Journey (Joseph Campbell / Christopher Vogler)

12 stages of mythic transformation. Best for epic adventure, coming-of-age, transformation stories.

### The 12 Stages

| # | Stage | Description |
|---|-------|-------------|
| 1 | Ordinary World | Hero's life before the adventure |
| 2 | Call to Adventure | Something disrupts the ordinary world |
| 3 | Refusal of the Call | Hero resists due to fear or doubt |
| 4 | Meeting the Mentor | Guidance arrives |
| 5 | Crossing the Threshold | Hero commits, enters the new world |
| 6 | Tests, Allies, Enemies | Hero learns the rules of the new world |
| 7 | Approach to the Inmost Cave | Preparation for the central ordeal |
| 8 | The Ordeal | Life-or-death crisis — confront greatest fear |
| 9 | The Reward | Something gained from surviving the ordeal |
| 10 | The Road Back | Consequences of the ordeal |
| 11 | The Resurrection | Final test — hero proves transformation |
| 12 | Return with the Elixir | Hero returns, changed, able to help others |

### Mapping to Save the Cat

| Save the Cat | Hero's Journey | Function |
|--------------|----------------|----------|
| Opening Image / Setup | Ordinary World | Establish normal life |
| Catalyst | Call to Adventure | Disruption begins |
| Debate | Refusal of the Call | Hero hesitates |
| Break into Two | Crossing the Threshold | Enter new world |
| Fun and Games | Tests, Allies, Enemies | Explore premise |
| Midpoint | Approach to Inmost Cave | Stakes escalate |
| All Is Lost | Ordeal | Major crisis |
| Break into Three | Reward | Insight gained |
| Finale | Resurrection | Final confrontation |
| Final Image | Return with Elixir | Transformed hero |

## Five-Act Structure (Freytag's Pyramid)

Classical structure. Climax in the middle. Best for tragedy, downfall arcs.

```
         CLIMAX (Act III)
            /\
           /  \
          /    \
    Rising/      \Falling
    Action        Action
        /          \
       /            \
Act I/              \Act IV
     /                \
    /                  \
Beginning            Ending
(Act V: Denouement)
```

### The 5 Acts

1. **Exposition** — Introduction, establish normal world
2. **Rising Action** — Complications, conflict builds
3. **Climax** — The turning point (MIDDLE of the story)
4. **Falling Action** — Consequences unfold
5. **Denouement** — Resolution, new normal

**Note:** Modern structure compresses the falling action and pushes the climax to ~90% of the way through. Freytag puts it in the center — which is why it works for tragedy (the fall IS the point) but not for most commercial films.

## Fichtean Curve

Chain of crises with minimal setup. Best for thrillers, horror, page-turners.

```
Crisis 1
  |  \
  |   Crisis 2
  |     |  \
  |     |   Crisis 3
  |     |     |  \
  |     |     |   Crisis 4
  |     |     |     |  \
  |     |     |     |   CLIMAX
  |     |     |     |     |
```

**Structure:**
- Minimal exposition — throw the reader into the action
- Each crisis is more intense than the last
- Brief recovery periods between crises (but tension never fully releases)
- The protagonist is always behind, always reacting
- The climax resolves ALL crises simultaneously

**Why it works for AI film:** Thrillers keep the audience hooked. Each crisis is a potential video clip with high visual drama.

## Seven-Point Story Structure

Plot backwards from a known ending. Best when you know the climax but not the middle.

### The 7 Points

1. **Hook** — The protagonist's starting state (opposite of resolution)
2. **Plot Turn 1** — The inciting incident (commits to the journey)
3. **Pinch Point 1** — Antagonist applies pressure
4. **Midpoint** — Protagonist shifts from reactive to proactive
5. **Pinch Point 2** — Antagonist applies more pressure
6. **Plot Turn 2** — Protagonist discovers the key to victory
7. **Resolution** — The climax — protagonist achieves (or fails) the goal

### Reverse Engineering

```
Start with: Resolution (what happens at the end?)
Then ask: What must happen before this? → Plot Turn 2
Then: What must happen before THAT? → Pinch Point 2
Continue backwards to Hook
```

## Sequence Method (Frank Daniel)

Breaks Act Two into manageable sequences. 8 sequences of 12-15 pages each.

### The 8 Sequences

| Sequence | Pages | What Happens |
|----------|-------|--------------|
| 1 | 1-15 | Setup + Inciting Incident + Debate |
| 2 | 15-30 | Break into Two + New World Exploration |
| 3 | 30-45 | Fun and Games (first half) |
| 4 | 45-60 | Midpoint + First Consequence |
| 5 | 60-75 | Fun and Games (second half) + Bad Guys Close In |
| 6 | 75-90 | All Is Lost + Dark Night |
| 7 | 90-105 | Break into Three + Finale |
| 8 | 105-110 | Final Image + Denouement |

**Each sequence is a mini-movie** with its own:
- Dramatic question
- Setup
- Development
- Climax

## Kishotenketsu

4-act structure from East Asian storytelling. No conflict engine. Best for quiet, literary, mood-driven stories.

1. **Ki** — Introduction
2. **Shō** — Development
3. **Ten** — Twist (not conflict — a new perspective)
4. **Ketsu** — Conclusion

**How it works:** Instead of building through conflict, the story develops through juxtaposition. The "twist" in Act Three recontextualizes everything without a confrontation. This produces contemplative, atmospheric films.

**AI filmmaking use:** Perfect for experimental short films, mood pieces, or visual poetry where atmosphere > plot.
